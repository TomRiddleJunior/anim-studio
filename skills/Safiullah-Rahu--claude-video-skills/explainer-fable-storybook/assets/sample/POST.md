## Caption (Instagram / TikTok)
The fox never asked for the key first. It asked for one paw.
In a 1966 study, people who had first been asked to show a tiny safe-driving sign were far more likely to accept a huge
one on their lawn two weeks later (76 % against 17 %). Psychologists call it the foot-in-the-door effect.
Which yes should the hens have refused?

## YouTube Shorts
**Title:** The Fox Who Only Asked for One Paw
**Description:** A short animated fable about the foot-in-the-door effect: a fox talks its way into a hen house one
small request at a time. The numbers come from Freedman & Fraser (1966), "Compliance without pressure: The
foot-in-the-door technique", Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 4(2), 195-202, Experiment II. Narration is an
AI voice; animation, music and sound are made in code.

## Music note
The full mix carries an original storybook bed (80 BPM, F major, music-box triplets over a pad; it darkens for the key
and turns plucky for the study). For a library track instead, upload `_nomusic.mp4` and add the track in the app (an
embedded commercial recording would be muted or claimed). Search the in-app library for "gentle piano storytelling",
"music box lullaby" or "whimsical curious pizzicato" around 75-85 BPM, keep it low under the voice (about -15 dB), and
pick one without a big drop.

## AI note
The narration is an AI voice (Kokoro text-to-speech). The characters, animation, music and sound effects are made in
code. The fox, the hens and the owl are invented; the study and its numbers are real (sources in FACTS.md).
