# "The Man Who Moved Every Clock" — fact sheet (checked 29 Oct 2026)

Every fact line in the narration maps to a row. Framing lines ("begged", "still waiting", "putting his idea away for
the winter") carry no new facts. Nothing here says Willett was laughed at — the sources only say Parliament considered
the idea and did nothing.

| # | Fact as used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | William Willett was a builder; he lived most of his life in Chislehurst, Kent (near London) | Wikipedia "William Willett" |
| F2 | The idea came to him after riding his horse in Petts Wood early one summer morning and seeing how many blinds were still down | Wikipedia "William Willett" |
| F3 | He self-funded the pamphlet "The Waste of Daylight" (July 1907) | Wikipedia "William Willett"; CNN via FOX 13 |
| F4 | It proposed advancing clocks 80 minutes in four 20-minute steps on successive Sundays in April, reversed in September | Wikipedia "William Willett" |
| F5 | He claimed it would save £2.5 million in lighting costs | Wikipedia "William Willett" |
| F6 | From 1908 MP Robert Pearce tried to pass it into law; Winston Churchill promoted it for a time; a select committee examined it in 1909; nothing was done | Wikipedia "William Willett"; Wikipedia "Daylight saving time" ("seriously considered by Parliament, it was not implemented until 1916") |
| F7 | Willett died of influenza on 4 March 1915, aged 58, before it became law | Wikipedia "William Willett" |
| F8 | The German Empire and Austria-Hungary introduced it nationwide on 30 April 1916, "as a way to conserve coal during wartime" — the first nationwide adoptions | Wikipedia "Daylight saving time" (History) |
| F9 | The UK first used it on 21 May 1916 (bill passed 17 May) — three weeks after Germany | Wikipedia "Daylight saving time"; Wikipedia "William Willett" |
| F10 | Moving clocks forward shifts waking hours into daylight, so evenings need less lighting (coal-fired) | general mechanism; F5, F8 |
| F11 | US daylight saving time ends Sunday 1 Nov 2026 at 2 a.m. (clocks fall back to 1 a.m.; not Arizona or Hawaii) | FOX 5 / Almanac, "When daylight saving time ends in 2026" |
