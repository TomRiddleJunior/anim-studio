Same resource, two results: Alaska's oil savings fund holds about US$88 billion, and Alberta's holds about a quarter of that.

Alaska's constitution sends at least 25% of oil royalties into the Permanent Fund, and the principal can only be invested.
Each year it sends about US$3.8 billion to the state budget for dividends and public services, and it is still growing.
Alberta took in about C$18 billion of resource revenue last year. Its Heritage Fund started in 1976 with a policy of saving 30% of that revenue.

Why the gap? Regular deposits stopped in 1987 and only restarted in 2006, and most of the fund's earnings (more than C$42 billion since it began) went into the provincial budget to help pay for services.
