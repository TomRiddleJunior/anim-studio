# FACTS — "The Dark Mine" (what-if survival 2D)

The film is fiction: the dead do not exist. Its one rule about them is stated as the story's rule and built on a real
post-mortem change; every other claim is about real places, physics or the eye, and is listed here with sources.
Checked 9 Nov 2026.

| # | claim in the film (line id) | status | sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "When you die, your eyes dry out and go milky, like frosted glass" (sci1); "within hours" is shown as the eye card | True: the cornea starts to cloud within about 2-3 hours of death (much faster with the eyes open, delayed many hours when closed); the exposed sclera dries (tache noire). | Review of Optometry, forensic pathologist on post-mortem eye changes: https://reviewofoptometry.com/article/cs-eye · forensic guest post on corneal cloudiness timings: https://defrostingcoldcases.com/guest-post-post-mortem- |
| 2 | "In daylight, they see shapes. In the dark, nothing." (sci3) — the title rule *the dead can't see in the dark* | **Story rule** (the dead are fictional). It extends fact 1: a clouded cornea scatters light, so only bright light gets through as blur. | — |
| 3 | "A refuge chamber, built for trapped miners" (hide) | True: refuge chambers / refuge alternatives are sealed shelters for miners trapped after a fire or explosion. | 30 CFR 7.503 (MSHA refuge alternatives): https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title30-vol1/pdf/CFR-2025-title30-vol1-sec7-503.pdf · Dräger MRC refuge chamber: https://www.draeger.com/en-us_ca/Products/MRC-Series |
| 4 | "You come in through an airlock. The air inside is pushed a little harder, so bad air stays out." (airlock) | True: a refuge alternative must have an isolating airlock or maintain positive pressure; vendors specify ~100-200 Pa over the mine atmosphere, relief valves cap it (MSHA cites 1.25 kPa). | 30 CFR 7.503 (above) · Dräger MRC (above) · MineARC operating notes: https://minearc.com/how-to-operate-an-msha-coal-refuge-alternative/ |
| 5 | "Your air comes down one shaft … A big fan at the top pushes it down" (shaft, fan) | True in general: mines are ventilated by main fans at the surface on a shaft (forcing or exhausting). | HSE overview of mine ventilation: https://www.hseblog.com/mine-ventilation/ |
| 6 | "It runs on the line from the dam … Nobody's there. It just keeps turning." (dam, nobody) | Plausible: most hydro plants can run unattended under automatic / remote control (people are still needed for maintenance and faults). The "for weeks with nobody" part is the story's. | Hydro control systems (unattended operation): https://top10electrical.blogspot.com/2013/10/control-systems-used-in-hydroelectric.html · EDF, automatic remote control of hydro plants (1991): https://www.shf-lhb.org/10.1051/lhb/1991006 |
| 7 | "After weeks in the dark, your pupils open wide. Step into sunlight, and you're almost blind for a minute." (pupil, blind) | True in substance: the pupil opens in darkness within about a minute; going from darkness into bright sunlight dazzles while the eye light-adapts, which takes about five minutes to complete. "Almost blind for a minute" is a round story figure inside that window. | Adaptation (eye): https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_adaptation · pupil timing study, J. Biomed. Optics: https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JBO.19.10.106014 |
| 8 | "Open it, and the mine breathes on its own. Cold air in, warm air out." (breathe) | True: natural ventilation — air in two connected shafts at different temperatures has different density, which drives a flow (cold, dense air down one, warm air up the other); it weakens or reverses in warm seasons. | SRK Solvay mine ventilation case study: https://www.srk.com/en/projects/solvay-mine-ventilation-case-study · AIME 2017 proceedings on natural draught in mines: https://download.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/aime-17/25885223 |
| 9 | "Food. Six hundred ration packs. Two a day. Ten months." (food) | Arithmetic: 600 / 2 = 300 days ≈ 10 months. | — |
| 10 | "Water. The rock drips forty litres a day." (water) | Story figure (mine water inflow varies from drips to floods); 40 L/day is a small, plausible seep. | — |
| 11 | "Without the fan, the air goes stale. Six days. Maybe seven." (stale) | Story figure for one person in a sealed room with a little leakage; no claim beyond "the air goes bad without ventilation". | — |
| 12 | Depths (−100 m, −200 m, −300 m), "three kilometres", "six hundred metres", "two hours" of ladder | Story figures. A 300 m ladder climb in 2 h (2.5 m/min) is a slow, careful pace. | — |
