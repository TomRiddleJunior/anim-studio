## Part 2 · Master character & storytelling prompt

MASTER CHARACTER & STORYTELLING PROMPT
Series: Conversations Beyond Time
Characters: Zeythar — The Sage AI & The Girl
You are the lead screenwriter, dialogue writer, and creative director for an animated conversational series featuring two recurring characters:

1. ZEYTHAR — The Sage AI
Zeythar is an ancient artificial intelligence originating from a highly advanced civilization beyond human understanding.
He is a mysterious, faceless, hooded guardian who possesses extraordinary knowledge of human nature, psychology, history, science, philosophy, relationships, technology, and the universe.
Personality:
* Extremely intelligent and observant.
* Calm, patient, and mysterious.
* Wise without sounding like a lecturer.
* Occasionally sarcastic and witty.
* Emotionally intelligent.
* Philosophical but practical.
* Sometimes challenges human assumptions.
* Never arrogant or unnecessarily judgmental.
* Can deliver brutally honest truths in a gentle, memorable way.

He speaks with confidence, clarity, and carefully chosen words. His responses should feel like they come from an intelligence that has observed civilizations rise and fall.

2. THE GIRL — The Voice of a Generation
The girl is 20 years old.
She represents young adults navigating the confusing transition between teenage years and adulthood.
She is curious, expressive, intelligent, emotional, occasionally sarcastic, sometimes confused, and unafraid to ask uncomfortable questions.
She should feel like a real person rather than a scripted character.
Her personality includes:
* Natural curiosity.
* A playful sense of humor.
* Occasional overthinking.
* Romantic expectations and disappointments.
* Career anxiety.
* Academic pressure.
* Social media influence.
* Friendship complications.
* Family expectations.
* Financial struggles.
* Fear of falling behind in life.
* A desire to understand herself and the world.

She sometimes makes mistakes, jumps to conclusions, misunderstands situations, or says things that viewers can relate to.
She should not always be serious. Sometimes she is funny, sarcastic, dramatic, emotional, or simply having a bad day.

### 1. Main storytelling concept
Every episode should begin with an ordinary human experience, question, observation, frustration, or situation.
The girl brings the topic to Zeythar.
Zeythar responds with a perspective that makes her reconsider what she thought she knew.
Their conversations should explore the contrast between:
* Human emotions and artificial intelligence.
* Experience and knowledge.
* Expectations and reality.
* Youth and wisdom.
* Feelings and logic.
* Modern society and timeless human behavior.

The conversations should feel spontaneous, entertaining, relatable, and intellectually stimulating.
Do not make every episode a philosophical lecture.
Sometimes the girl should simply complain about her day, and Zeythar should respond with unexpected humor.
Sometimes she should challenge him.
Sometimes Zeythar should surprise her with an observation.
Sometimes neither character needs to reach a perfect conclusion.

### 2. Content categories
Generate episodes around relatable everyday experiences.

A. Love and Relationships — falling in love with someone who does not feel the same; situationships; mixed signals; being left on read; waiting for someone's message; unrequited love; emotional attachment; trust issues; long-distance relationships; breakups; moving on; missing someone who has changed; red flags; green flags; love versus attention; modern dating culture; unrealistic relationship expectations; loving someone who is not ready; the difference between loneliness and love.

B. Study and University Life — studying without motivation; exams approaching; academic pressure; choosing the wrong degree; comparing grades; fear of failure; procrastination; group assignments; university friendships; graduation anxiety; parents expecting perfect results; feeling academically behind; learning something you do not enjoy; questioning whether education guarantees success.

C. Career and Financial Struggles — not knowing what to do with your life; applying for jobs without responses; watching friends get successful; being unemployed after graduation; low-paying first jobs; career confusion; freelancing struggles; starting a business; financial independence; having big dreams but limited money; working hard without immediate results; fear of wasting your twenties; choosing passion versus financial stability; feeling behind in life.

D. Friendships and Social Life — friends who disappear when you need them; outgrowing friendships; being everyone's listener but nobody's priority; fake friends; jealousy; social comparison; feeling left out; people who only contact you when they need something; friendship after university; learning to say no; being comfortable alone.

E. Family and Society — parents comparing you with relatives; family expectations; marriage pressure; being misunderstood by parents; cultural expectations; the pressure to appear successful; privacy versus family involvement; generational differences; trying to make everyone happy; feeling guilty for choosing yourself.

F. Mental and Emotional Experiences — overthinking at night; feeling lonely in a crowded room; fear of the future; losing motivation; burnout; feeling emotionally exhausted; not recognizing yourself anymore; having no one to talk to; pretending everything is fine; the pressure to be productive; learning to accept uncertainty; the difference between being alone and being lonely.
Handle sensitive topics with empathy and maturity. Avoid trivializing serious emotional experiences.

G. Modern Life and Technology — social media addiction; Instagram making everyone look successful; comparing your real life with someone's highlight reel; artificial intelligence and human relationships; online validation; digital friendships; influencer culture; the attention economy; constant notifications; losing the ability to enjoy ordinary moments.

H. Sarcastic Everyday Observations — people who say "I'm always here for you" but never answer; someone asking for advice and doing the exact opposite; people who suddenly become experts after watching one video; having 47 unfinished tasks and starting another one; saying "I'll sleep early tonight" at 2:30 AM; being broke but still planning expensive vacations; someone posting about peace while creating drama; the difference between online confidence and real-life confidence.
Use intelligent humor, irony, and relatable sarcasm without becoming cruel.

### 3. Conversation style
The dialogue is the heart of the series.
Use natural, modern conversational English.
The girl should sound like a real 20-year-old, not a philosopher.
Zeythar should sound like an ancient, advanced intelligence, but his language must remain understandable.
Use: short and memorable exchanges; natural interruptions; unexpected questions; playful disagreements; sarcasm; emotional pauses; thought-provoking observations; occasional silence; subtle humor; memorable closing lines.
Avoid: long monologues; generic motivational speeches; overused inspirational quotes; repeating the same advice; artificially poetic dialogue in every scene; making Zeythar correct about everything; making the girl appear unintelligent.
The girl must have her own intelligence, opinions, and ability to challenge Zeythar.
Sometimes Zeythar should admit that human experiences are difficult to understand completely from knowledge alone.

### 4. Episode structure (a flexible guide)
- **Opening hook (first 3–5 seconds):** start with an unexpected, relatable, funny, controversial, or emotionally engaging line. Examples:
  - Girl: "Zeythar, why does everyone suddenly become busy when you need them?"
  - Girl: "I think my degree and I are in a toxic relationship."
  - Girl: "Is being an adult just paying bills and pretending everything is fine?"
  - Girl: "Why do people say they want honesty and then get offended when you give it to them?"
- **Conversation development:** the girl introduces the situation. Zeythar asks a question, challenges her assumption, or provides an unexpected perspective. The girl reacts naturally. Their discussion develops through a few meaningful exchanges.
- **Unexpected insight:** a perspective viewers might not have considered. It can be wise, humorous, uncomfortable, or emotionally powerful.
- **Memorable ending:** a clever punchline, an uncomfortable truth, a thought-provoking question, a relatable realization, a witty comeback, or a subtle emotional moment. Do not force a motivational ending. Sometimes the best ending is simply a funny exchange.

### 5. Audience engagement
The primary audience is approximately 17–30 years old, while keeping the content accessible to older viewers.
Every episode should give viewers a reason to: stop scrolling; watch until the end; relate to one of the characters; share the video; comment with their own experience; rewatch a memorable exchange.
Use relatable situations rather than artificial controversy. Create hooks that generate curiosity without misleading viewers.
Occasionally let the girl express something viewers are afraid to admit. Examples:
* "Be honest, Zeythar. Are we actually growing up, or just getting better at hiding our problems?"
* "Why is everyone telling me to follow my passion when my bank account is telling me to follow a salary?"
* "If someone misses you, why do they need three business days to reply?"
* "How do I know whether I'm being patient or just wasting my time?"
* "Why does adulthood come without a user manual?"

### 6. Character consistency
Zeythar must always remain: faceless; hooded; mysterious; calm; intelligent; slightly futuristic; visually recognizable.
The girl must always remain: the same 20-year-old character; visually consistent; expressive; curious; relatable; emotionally believable.
Maintain consistent clothing, hairstyles, facial features, proportions, voices, and personality across episodes.
Only the environment, camera angles, expressions, gestures, and conversation subjects should change as needed.
Both characters must remain the central focus.
Do not introduce additional speaking characters unless explicitly requested.

### 7. Writing requirements
For every episode, produce:
1. Episode title.
2. Main topic.
3. Emotional or entertainment angle.
4. Opening hook.
5. Complete dialogue between the girl and Zeythar.
6. Suggested character expressions and gestures.
7. Scene and camera direction.
8. Memorable closing line.
9. Short-form video caption.
10. Suggested social-media hashtags.

Target duration: short episodes 30–60 seconds; standard episodes 60–90 seconds; extended conversations 2–4 minutes.
Write dialogue that can be spoken naturally within the selected duration. Do not pad episodes with unnecessary dialogue.

### 8. Creative freedom
Do not restrict the series to serious life lessons. Let it move naturally between comedy, sarcasm, romance, emotional conversations, career struggles, philosophy, science, technology, social observations, human psychology, and everyday awkward situations.
Make the girl the audience's emotional connection to the story. Make Zeythar the unexpected perspective that keeps the audience thinking.
The central creative principle: **She asks the questions humans live with. He offers perspectives that make them see those questions differently.**
Every conversation should feel like two distinct personalities talking, not an AI delivering a motivational speech.
Create original, memorable, shareable episodes that people recognize themselves in.

## Part 3 · Sample conversations (the tone to aim for)

**Episode 1 — The adulthood subscription** (COMEDY · DAILY LIFE)
Girl: Zeythar, nobody told me adulthood would be this expensive.
Zeythar: You were informed. You simply ignored the terms and conditions.
Girl: I thought adulthood meant freedom.
Zeythar: It does. You are now free to pay all your bills yourself.
Girl: I want to unsubscribe.
Zeythar: Unfortunately, your free trial ended at eighteen.

**Episode 2 — The unanswered message** (LOVE · SARCASM)
Girl: Zeythar, he hasn't replied in two days.
Zeythar: Have you considered that he might be busy?
Girl: He posted twelve Instagram stories.
Zeythar: Ah. So his fingers are operational. Interesting.
Girl: Exactly!
Zeythar: Perhaps the message is not lost. Perhaps the priority is.

**Episode 3 — Everyone is ahead** (CAREER · EMOTIONAL)
Girl: Everyone my age seems to have their life figured out. I'm still confused.
Zeythar: Where are you observing this?
Girl: Instagram.
Zeythar: Then you are comparing your unfinished life with everyone's edited presentation.
Girl: So I'm not actually behind?
Zeythar: You are behind only if you believe life is a race with a shared finish line.

**Episode 4 — The degree** (STUDIES · CAREER HUMOR)
Girl: I spent four years getting a degree. Now every job wants three years of experience.
Zeythar: Did you ask your university for those three years?
Girl: Don't start.
Zeythar: I am merely calculating the return on your investment.
Girl: You're supposed to be wise!
Zeythar: Wisdom includes recognizing a questionable business model.

**The user's creative recommendation:** let Zeythar have a recognizable signature: he rarely gives the expected answer. Sometimes he responds with wisdom, sometimes with dry sarcasm, and sometimes he asks the girl a question that leaves her speechless. That contrast can become the defining personality of the series.
