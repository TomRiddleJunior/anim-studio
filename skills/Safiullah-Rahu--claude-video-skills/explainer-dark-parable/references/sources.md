## Facts used in the sample film (The Rope)
- Seligman, M. E. P., & Maier, S. F. (1967). Failure to escape traumatic shock. *Journal of Experimental Psychology, 74*(1), 1–9.
  https://doi.org/10.1037/h0024514 (PubMed 6032570)
- Maier, S. F., & Seligman, M. E. P. (2016). Learned helplessness at fifty: Insights from neuroscience. *Psychological Review,
  123*(4), 349–367. https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000033 (PubMed 27337390, PMC4920136). The abstract states that the original
  theory "got it backward": passivity is the default, unlearned response to prolonged aversive events, and what is learned is control.
- Baratta, Seligman & Maier (2023). From helplessness to controllability: toward a neuroscience of resilience. *Frontiers in
  Psychiatry, 14*, 1170417. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1170417 (background)
- Secondary descriptions of the shuttle-box procedure: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_helplessness and
  https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/lhtheoryevidence.pdf. Sources disagree on the barrier's height, so the film
  says only "one jump over a barrier".
- Asian elephant adult female mass: about 2,000–3,500 kg in general references, so "three tons" is in range. The elephant-and-rope
