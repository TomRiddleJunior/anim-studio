# Facts behind "The Pothole Calendar" (satire-cartoon sample)

Everything in the film is fictional except one real economics term.

| claim in the VO / on screen | status | source |
|---|---|---|
| "Economists even have a name for it: the political budget cycle." | **Real term.** Incumbents tend to raise (especially visible) spending or cut taxes before elections to improve their re-election chances. | K. Rogoff (1990), "Equilibrium Political Budget Cycles", *American Economic Review* 80(1): 21–36 · K. Rogoff & A. Sibert (1988), "Elections and Macroeconomic Policy Cycles", *Review of Economic Studies* 55(1): 1–16 · A. Brender & A. Drazen (2005), "Political budget cycles in new versus established democracies", *Journal of Monetary Economics* 52(7): 1271–1295 · related: W. Nordhaus (1975), "The Political Business Cycle", *Review of Economic Studies* 42(2): 169–190 |
| Port Fennick, Mayor Bunbury, Gus of Smoothly Paving, the Roads Depot, the election on November 5th, "a fifth term", "four more years" | Fictional town, people, company, dates and terms. | — |
| The steamroller "sleeps under a tarp eleven months a year", "two frantic weeks paving anything that holds still", the speech dated NOV 7 → NOV 5, the slogan "re-elect me, or keep the potholes" | Fictional, inside the story. | — |
| Pothole counter 3 → 7 → 19 → 212 → 2,191; FIXED THIS WEEK 0 → 400 → 0; repair bill $0 → $2,191.73 | Fictional joke numbers inside a fictional town (the style's absurd price-sign counters). No real-world statistic is claimed. | — |
| "Fix a road in March and nobody remembers by November" | A joke about voter memory, not a statistic. | — |

No real, living person is depicted, named or quoted. All characters, logos (the anchor-and-fennel crest), shops
(TYRES & BIKES, BAKERY) and signs are original.
